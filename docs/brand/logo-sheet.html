Ledger Tracker logo kit

Built from the four vector files supplied by the owner. The drawing is theirs, untouched. The only thing that changes between files is the ink on the page edges and base line, plus an optional background tile, so the mark stays visible on any surface, with or without a background.

The files

Two transparent versions for flat surfaces, two tiles for everywhere else. All live in public/brand/, with PNGs at 512 px.

Which one, where

Flat light surface: the light mark. Flat dark surface: the dark mark. Anything with a picture, a gradient, glass, or a ground you do not control (partner pages, avatars, embeds): the tile.

What not to do

Wrong ink, or a bare mark where the ground fights it.

Small sizes

The tile at tab-icon and avatar sizes, on a light and a dark ground. The page edges are about 4% of the mark's width, so below 24 px they soften. That is normal for this drawing and the tile keeps it readable; do not thicken it.

Measured contrast

WCAG ratio of each colour against the surface it sits on. Logos are exempt from the rule, but 3:1 is a fair bar for "visible".

Wrong-way ink: dark on Vault 1.2:1, white on Pearl 1.07:1, white on a white tile 1.0:1. That is why the ink switches with the surface.

Usage rules (proposed defaults, not supplied by the owner)

  • Do not redraw, recolour, stretch, rotate, outline or add effects to the mark. The only variable is the ink.
  • Do not put the mark in a gradient or a second colour.
  • Keep clear space of at least a quarter of the mark's height on every side.
  • Do not use the approximated marks from earlier in the project; they are replaced everywhere.

Still missing: the wordmark

The supplied files contain "LEDGER TRACKER" as live text in the font Satoshi Black, with no font embedded and no outlines, so no lockup is shipped yet.

On a machine without Satoshi the letters fall back to another font and the spacing breaks. And in all four supplied files the words are white, so on the two light-surface versions the wordmark is invisible. Ask the designer for the same four files with the text converted to outlines, and with a dark (#231f20) wordmark in the two light versions.
